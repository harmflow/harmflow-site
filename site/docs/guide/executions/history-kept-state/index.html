What History keeps until you quit

The History screen remembers what you were looking at. Set its filters, load older executions, go to another screen, and come back: the rows and the filters are still there. This page says what History keeps, how it brings what it kept up to date, and what a new launch of the app shows.

What it keeps

While the app runs, History keeps:

  • its rows, including the ones you added with Load older executions;
  • its count of loaded executions out of the total, such as Showing 100 / 240;
  • its filters: the search text, Status, Task, Trigger, and the From and To dates.

When you leave History and come back, it shows all of them again at once, with no empty list while it loads. Closing the window while HarmFlow shows in the menu bar only hides it, so History keeps them then too; the menu bar options are in The Settings screen.

Coming back to History

On coming back, History checks whether anything happened since it last fetched its rows. If an execution changed meanwhile, History fetches its rows again in the background, under the same filters, while it shows the ones it kept. An execution that ended while you were away then shows its new state, new executions come in at the top, and the rows you added with Load older executions stay. If nothing changed, History shows what it kept and asks the daemon for nothing.

Changing a filter starts again from the first page, since the rows loaded belong to the filters you had before.

When a refresh fails, or the connection is lost

If the background refresh fails, the rows and the count you had stay on the screen, and the connection banner tells you about the problem. History asks again the next time you open it.

When the app's live connection to the daemon was lost, it may have missed events. Once the connection is back, the next time you open History it fetches its rows again, even if no new event arrived.

After you quit the app

Quitting the app drops all of it. So does closing its window while HarmFlow does not show in the menu bar, which ends the app as quitting does; the daemon and its executions run on either way. A new launch opens History with no filter set and its first page of executions, as on a first visit. History keeps its rows and filters in memory only: nothing of it is saved to disk.
